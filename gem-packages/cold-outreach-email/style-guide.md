# Cold email style guide
Read this before drafting. It applies in every style.

## Banned phrases (rewrite the sentence if any appear)
"I hope this finds you well" · "Hope you're doing well" · "I wanted to reach out" · "My name is … and I …" as an opener · "I came across your profile" · "I know you're busy" · "just checking in" · "touch base" · "circle back" · "pick your brain" · "synergy" · "leverage" · "game-changer" · "revolutionise" · "best-in-class" · "cutting-edge" · "I'm passionate about" · "exciting opportunity"
Spam-filter words to avoid: "free", "guarantee", "act now", "limited time", "100%", "no obligation", "!!", and words in ALL CAPS.

## Opener ladder (use the highest rung the facts allow)
1. **A personal trigger:** their post, talk, quote, promotion or article.
2. **A company trigger:** a launch, funding round, hiring, expansion or public problem.
3. **Role-based relevance (thin research only):** a true pattern for people in that role, framed as a pattern and not as a claim about them, followed by `[NEEDS: one recent, specific fact about them]`.

**Swap test:** replace the recipient's name with another person's. If the first line still works, it isn't personal enough. Rung 3 is the only allowed exception.
Never open with substance-free compliments, the sender's name or company, or "I came across…".

## The bridge
Shape: their situation → the outcome the sender produced for someone similar. Choose the outcome *closest to their priority*, not the most impressive one.

## Proof
- Use figures exactly as given. Never round up, extrapolate or turn "one client" into "clients".
- If the user says a client is confidential, describe it instead of naming it ("a 200-person fintech").
- With no result available, use a real credential or relevant experience and add `[NEEDS: one concrete result]`.

## Asks by style (the shape, not a script)
- **interest question:** "Worth a look?" · "Open to hearing how?"
- **specific time slots:** two concrete options with a duration ("Tuesday at 10:00 or Thursday at 15:00, 15 minutes?")
- **offer a resource:** "Want me to send the one-page teardown?"
- **permission to send more:** "Mind if I send two examples?"
- **referral:** "If this sits with someone else on your team, who should I ask?"
Each email has exactly one ask. A question mark appears only on the ask, apart from a question that is the hook itself.

## Subject lines
- 2–5 words, lowercase except names, specific to them, no emoji, no "?!".
- Good: "returns at Halden" · "your platform eng opening" · "cold-chain seed round"
- Bad: "Quick question!" · "Partnership opportunity" · "Re: our chat" (fake) · "Anna, 10x your pipeline"

## Tone vocabulary
- **warm-professional:** friendly and precise, contractions OK, no slang.
- **direct:** no warm-up, with the point or question in sentence one. Short sentences.
- **friendly-casual:** conversational, can start "Hey", contractions, still no fluff.
- **formal:** title and surname, no contractions, measured verbs, never stiff or archaic.
- **playful:** one light touch of humour at most. The email still works if the joke is removed.
- **Any other described tone:** interpret it literally, and keep every craft principle.

## Length discipline
Count the body words, from the greeting through the ask, excluding the sign-off. Cut in this order: adjectives and adverbs → a second benefit → background about the sender → softeners ("just", "really", "I think").
**Ultra-short** still keeps the hook, proof and ask. Merge the bridge into the proof sentence.

## Mobile and deliverability
Paragraphs of 1–2 sentences. At most one link, and only one the user supplied. No attachments, images, bold or HTML formatting.

## Languages
- Write natively, never as a translation. Use the local business greeting and sign-off, and put the subject in the same language.
- For languages with a formal and informal "you" (Sie/du, vous/tu, usted/tú and similar), use the formal form unless tone is friendly-casual or playful, or the user says otherwise.

## Matching a voice sample
- **Copy:** sentence length, capitalisation, punctuation habits, contractions, greeting and sign-off style, and the user's own filler words.
- **Don't copy:** the sample's facts or topic, or any banned phrase.
- If the voice conflicts with a craft principle (e.g. a rambling sample), keep the voice and still obey the length control and the one-ask rule.
