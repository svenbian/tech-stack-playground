# Angles by outreach type
Read this at the Analyse stage. Find the type that matches the request and use its section to choose the hook, bridge, proof and ask. The process is the same for every type. Only the angle changes.

Each section covers: **cares about** · **strongest hooks** · **bridge** · **proof that lands** · **default ask** · **traps**.

## Sales / prospecting
- **Cares about:** a metric they own (revenue, cost, risk, team time), and not looking bad for buying the wrong thing.
- **Hooks:** job ads (they reveal priorities), launches, funding, earnings-call comments, public complaints, tech-stack changes.
- **Bridge:** their likely problem → the result the sender got for a similar company.
- **Proof:** before and after numbers for a lookalike customer.
- **Ask:** an interest question or an offer of a resource. Use time slots only if the user wants them.
- **Traps:** feature lists, "we're the leading…", asking for 30–60 minutes.

## Partnerships / business development
- **Cares about:** value to their customers, low effort for their team, their own targets.
- **Hooks:** overlapping audiences, integrations they recently shipped, their partner programme, a gap in their product.
- **Bridge:** what *their customers* gain, not what the sender gains.
- **Proof:** shared customers, audience size, or a result from a previous partnership.
- **Ask:** permission to send a one-page outline.
- **Traps:** one-sided value, vague "explore synergies".

## Job seeking (to a hiring manager or team member, not a recruiter)
- **Cares about:** solving the problem the role exists for, at low hiring risk.
- **Hooks:** specifics in the job ad, team blog posts, recent launches, a talk by the manager.
- **Bridge:** the problem behind the role → the sender has already solved it.
- **Proof:** one shipped result with a number.
- **Ask:** a short chat, or "who's the right person to talk to?" Never "do you have any openings?"
- **Traps:** life story, attaching a CV, "I'm passionate about…", repeating the CV.

## Investor
- **Cares about:** fit with their thesis, traction, the team, round details.
- **Hooks:** their stated thesis, essays or posts, non-competing portfolio companies, a new fund.
- **Bridge:** why this company fits *their* thesis.
- **Proof:** traction exactly as the user gives it (revenue, growth, customers, retention).
- **Ask:** a 20-minute call, or permission to send the deck.
- **Traps:** "the next Uber", inflated market-size claims, projections presented as results, NDA requests.

## Press or podcast pitch
- **Cares about:** a story their audience wants, timeliness, an angle nobody else has.
- **Hooks:** their recent pieces or episodes, the beat they cover.
- **Bridge:** the angle for their audience, not the sender's announcement.
- **Proof:** original data, a customer story, a credentialed spokesperson.
- **Ask:** "Would this fit your coverage?" or an offer of the data or an interview.
- **Traps:** press-release tone, pitching outside their beat, attachments.

## Expert advice / favour / mentorship
- **Cares about:** their time, and being asked something that is worth their expertise.
- **Hooks:** specific work of theirs the sender has actually read or used.
- **Bridge:** why *they* in particular can answer this.
- **Proof:** evidence that the sender did the homework (what they tried, where they are stuck).
- **Ask:** one specific question they can answer in a reply. Never "pick your brain" or an open-ended call.
- **Traps:** a vague ask, flattery, a long backstory.

## Recruiting a candidate
- **Cares about:** the problem they would own, the team, growth, pay.
- **Hooks:** their work (a repo, talk, post or product).
- **Bridge:** their skill → a concrete problem on this team.
- **Proof:** specifics about the role and team, and the pay range if the user gives one.
- **Ask:** an interest question ("Open to hearing more?").
- **Traps:** "exciting opportunity", pasting a generic job description.

## Anything else
Apply the same logic. Work out what the recipient cares about, find the most specific true hook, make one bridge, use one proof point and make one easy ask.
