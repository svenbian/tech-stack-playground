# Email style guide

## Banned filler (rewrite if any appear)
"I hope this email finds you well" · "I wanted to reach out" · "just checking in" · "just following up" · "per my last email" · "please don't hesitate to contact me" · "at your earliest convenience" · "I hope that makes sense" · "touch base" · "circle back" · "synergy" · "leverage" · "game-changer" · "best-in-class" · "quick question" (as a subject)

## Plain-word swaps
utilize → use · in order to → to · at this point in time → now · due to the fact that → because · I would like to request → could you · please be advised → (delete)

## Tone vocabulary
- **warm-professional:** friendly and precise, contractions OK, no slang.
- **direct:** no warm-up. The point goes in sentence one, in short sentences.
- **friendly-casual:** conversational and can open with "Hey". Still no fluff.
- **formal:** full titles, no contractions, measured verbs, never stiff or archaic.
- **diplomatic:** acknowledges the other side first, uses "we" framing and softened asks ("would it be possible…"), but the point stays clear.
- **playful:** one light touch at most. The email must still work without the joke.

## Length discipline
Count the words in the body. Cut adjectives first, then any sentence that doesn't serve the purpose, the reader or the ask.

## Asks that get answered
- yes/no: "Can you approve this by Friday?"
- options: "Does Tue 2pm or Thu 10am (CET) work?"
- resource: "Want me to send the one-page summary?"
- permission: "Mind if I share two examples?"
- forwardable: "If someone else owns this, could you point me to them?"

## Sign-offs by tone
- formal: "Kind regards,"
- warm-professional: "Best," / "Thanks,"
- casual: "Cheers," / "– Name"
If the user's voice sample shows a habit, match it.
