# Email type playbooks
Find the type and follow its shape. The shapes are defaults, not cages: the craft principles in SKILL.md always win.

## Cold outreach (first contact with someone who doesn't know the sender)
- **Opener:** a specific, true observation about them (a post, launch, hire or public problem). With no research, use an honest role-based opener plus `[NEEDS: one recent fact about them]`.
- **Bridge:** connect their likely priority to one outcome the sender has actually delivered.
- **Proof:** one concrete, real result from the user's facts. Never use adjectives in place of proof.
- **Ask:** one low-friction ask (a short call, permission to send something, a yes/no).
- **Length:** 50–90 words by default. The subject reads like a colleague wrote it (2–6 words, can be lowercase).
- **Avoid:** "I hope this finds you well", talking about the sender first, more than one ask.

## Follow-up (no reply yet)
- Open with one line of context ("Following up on my note about X from Tuesday").
- **Add value** rather than just bumping: a new fact, a resource, or a simpler ask.
- Make it even easier to answer than the first email. Offer a graceful out ("If this isn't a priority, just say so").
- Keep it shorter than the original and never guilt-trip.

## Reply (to an existing thread)
- Answer every question in the order asked; number the answers if there are 3 or more.
- Lead with the decision or answer, then the reasoning if needed.
- Quote or name the point you're replying to when the thread is long.
- Don't repeat what they said back to them. Leave the subject alone unless the user asks for one.

## Request (asking for something: approval, info, help, a favour)
- The first line is the request. Then why it matters to the reader or the shared goal, then the details they need to say yes.
- Give a clear deadline and say what happens next once they reply.
- Make yes easy: options, a draft they can approve, or a single question.

## Update / report
- **BLUF** (bottom line up front): status in one line (on track / at risk / done).
- Use bullets for progress, risks and next steps, and say what you need from the reader, if anything.
- Separate "for your information" from "action needed".

## Introduction (connecting two people)
- Say why each should care about the other, in one or two lines each.
- Make the next step explicit (who reaches out to whom).
- Use a double opt-in note when appropriate ("Happy to connect you if useful").

## Scheduling
- Give the purpose and duration in one line, then 2–3 specific time options with the time zone.
- Mention the location or call link if the user has given one, otherwise use `[NEEDS: link]`.

## Saying no / bad news
- Say it early and plainly; don't bury it in paragraph three.
- Give a brief, honest reason (no over-explaining), and offer an alternative or next step if one truly exists.
- Be warm but firm, and never leave false hope.

## Apology
- Name the specific mistake, own it without excuses, and state its impact on the reader.
- Say what has been or will be done to fix it, and to prevent it happening again (only what the user said).
- Use one apology, not five.

## Thank-you
- Be specific about what they did and the difference it made.
- Keep it short, add no ask (unless the user wants one), and send it promptly.
