# Examples: illustrations of quality, never copy their content
The people, companies and figures below are fictional. They show the *shape* of a great email at different settings. Never reuse their wording, facts or structure word for word. Always build from the user's facts.

## 1 · sales · warm-professional · short · interest question
**Subject:** returns at Halden

Hi Joanna,

You mentioned on Halden's Q2 call that returns now cost more than shipping. That's usually a sizing-data problem, not a policy one.

We added fit prediction to Corra's product pages last year, and their return rate fell from 24% to 15% in two quarters.

Worth a 15-minute look at whether the same gap exists on yours?

Best,
Marcus

*Why it works:* public trigger, one diagnosis, lookalike proof with exact numbers, easy yes or no.

## 2 · job seeking · friendly-casual · ultra-short · interest question
**Subject:** your platform eng opening

Hey Priyanka,

Saw Tidepool is hiring a platform engineer to tame Kafka on-call. I did exactly that at Brightline: pages dropped from about 30 a week to 4.

Open to a quick chat before I apply formally?

– Leo

*Why it works:* the job ad is the hook, one result proves fit, and the ask is small.

## 3 · investor · formal · medium · specific time slots
**Subject:** cold-chain seed round

Dear Ms Okafor,

Your recent essay arguing that cold-chain software still runs on spreadsheets describes precisely the problem we are solving.

Frostline gives mid-sized food distributors real-time temperature tracking across their own and third-party fleets. Since launching in March, we have signed 14 paying distributors and reached £38,000 in monthly recurring revenue, without losing a customer.

We are raising a £1.5m seed round and would value your perspective, given your investments in Haulr and Pallet. Would Tuesday at 10:00 or Thursday at 15:00 next week suit you for a 20-minute call?

Kind regards,
Daniel Achebe
Founder, Frostline

*Why it works:* matches her thesis, exact traction, no hype, two concrete slots.

## 4 · expert favour · direct · short · one specific question
**Subject:** one SOC 2 scoping question

Hi Dr Mensah,

Should a 6-person fintech put its support tooling inside the SOC 2 boundary?

Your FinSecCon talk on keeping the boundary small is the clearest thing I've found on scoping, and this is the one decision we keep going back and forth on. A one-line answer would be plenty.

Thanks,
Aiko Tanaka
CTO, Ledgerly

*Why it works:* the question comes first, shows real homework, and the ask is tiny and specific.

## 5 · partnership · thin research · warm-professional · permission to send more
**Subject:** day-one laptops for Brightpath clients

Hi Sofia,

Payroll platforms often hear the same complaint from clients: new hires wait days for a working laptop.

Kitwise ships pre-configured equipment that arrives before day one. Fernway's clients went from a 4-day wait to same-day logins.

Mind if I send a one-page outline of how a Brightpath integration could work?

Best,
Nadia

[NEEDS: one recent, specific fact about Sofia or Brightpath (a launch, post or hire) to replace the role-based opener.]

*Why it works:* an honest pattern opener instead of invented research, real proof, and the gap flagged.

## Anti-example (what not to do)
**Subject:** Quick question!!

Hi there, I hope this finds you well! My name is Jake and I came across your profile and was really impressed. We're a best-in-class agency that leverages cutting-edge solutions to help companies like yours grow. Would you have 30 minutes this week for a call, or could you point me to the right person?

*Why it fails:* generic opener, about the sender, buzzwords and spam words, no proof, two asks including a big one, and a marketing-style subject.
